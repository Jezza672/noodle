# Tempo sync is a source node, not a mode on each node

Delay's `time` is in seconds and the LFO's `rate` is in hertz. To make either follow the project's tempo (#142) we add one primitive, **Tempo** (`noodle.mod.tempo`), instead of a `sync` setting on each node that has a time or a rate. Tempo has a `division` (Clock's divisions of the whole note, so `1/1` is four beats whatever the time signature, plus dotted values and multiples of `1/1`; Clock and Tempo share one table of division lengths in ticks) and two control outputs that follow the transport's tempo: `time`, the division's length in seconds, and `rate`, its reciprocal in hertz. Wire `time` into a Delay's `time`, `rate` into an LFO's `rate`, or either into anything else with a seconds or hertz parameter (a chorus, an envelope's length, a Lag).

A `sync` config on Delay and LFO was the other option in the issue. It would change what those nodes' parameters mean, give every future time-based node its own copy of the beat arithmetic and division list, and still leave a synth with no way to sync a node that did not get the option. One source node is the same pattern Clock already uses, keeps Delay and LFO as plain primitives, and lets a group expose just a `division` control. A synced echo and a synced tremolo are therefore groups (templates in the library), editable like any other.

The cost is that the tempo reaches a node as a signal that is constant within a block: a tempo change inside a block lands at the next block, like Clock and the Metronome, and a Delay whose `time` jumps with the tempo jumps in one step (a click on the echo's tail while the tempo moves; a tempo ramp is a staircase of block-sized steps, about 2.7 ms at the app's block). Putting a Lag between `time` and the Delay smooths this and is what the echo template does. Tempo is stateless, so a rebuilt copy picks up where the old one was, and it works while the transport is stopped (it reads the tempo at the transport's position, 120 bpm by default).

Delay's line holds at most 2 seconds, which `1/1` reaches at 120 bpm and longer divisions or slower tempos pass. A Delay clamps a longer `time` to its maximum, so past that the echo template plays a 2-second echo that is off the beat; raising Delay's maximum (it is allocated up front) is a separate decision if a user needs it.

Delay's `time` and the LFO's `rate` are offset sliders: a wire moves the slider along its travel (0 to 2 s; 0.01 to 100 Hz on a log taper) instead of setting the value. Tempo's outputs are the values themselves, so the templates convert them with a Math node (`a / 2` for the echo, `ln(a * 100) / ln(10000)` for the tremolo) and leave the slider at its minimum. A Math input and a Lag's times take a wire as the value, so those need no conversion.
